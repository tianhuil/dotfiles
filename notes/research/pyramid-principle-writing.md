# Pyramid Principle for writing

## Summary

Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle (developed at McKinsey) says: state the answer first, then group supporting ideas under it so each level summarizes the one below. It is distilled into the `pyramid-writing` skill at `home/agents/.agents/skills/pyramid-writing/SKILL.md`.

- Three rules: ideas at any level summarize the ideas grouped below; ideas in a group are the same kind; ideas in a group are logically ordered (time, structure, or rank).
- Vertically, each idea raises a question the level below answers. Horizontally, prefer inductive groups over deductive chains at upper levels.
- Introductions follow Situation → Complication → Question → Answer (SCQA); groups should be MECE.

## Details

- **Purpose** (Minto's site): "a process for organizing your thinking so a reader can absorb it quickly." Thinking groups low-level facts into insights, then insights into one central thought; "Communicating the thinking requires only that you guide the reader down the pyramid." Applies to emails, memos, reports, and slides.
- **Rules** (Huehn summary, quoting the book): "Ideas must be summaries of ideas grouped below"; "Ideas grouped together must be of 'same kind'"; "Ideas grouped together must be logically ordered." Minto: "You can't derive an idea from a grouping unless the ideas in the grouping are logically the same, and in logical order" (StrategyU).
- **Question/answer**: an idea raises a question in the reader's mind; don't raise a question you can't answer, and don't answer questions the reader hasn't raised (Huehn).
- **Inductive vs deductive**: deductive chains suit lower levels and are "ponderous" at the key-line level; inductive groups are the default higher up, named by a plural noun (Huehn).
- **Order**: time, structural, or ranking order; causes precede effects (Huehn).
- **SCQA**: Situation is context the reader already accepts; Complication is a change (not necessarily a problem) that prompts writing; Question follows from it; Answer is the governing thought (Huehn).
- **Insight over labels**: "There are two problems" names a kind but summarizes nothing; headings like "Findings" have no scanning value (Huehn).
- **Governing thought is a claim, not a topic**: "This report covers our options for market entry" vs "We should enter the market through acquisition" (secondary summaries).
